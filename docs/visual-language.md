# Visual standard

SVG and GL represent the same specimen in different media. They should share anatomy, proportions, pigment landmarks and orientation without sharing pixels.

## Shared facts

Both renderers preserve:

- organ identities, counts, hierarchy and order;
- repeated-organ geometry and symmetry;
- silhouette landmarks and relative proportions;
- throats, margins, bands, midribs and freckles;
- tissue thickness, softness and translucency;
- framing and preferred light direction.

Grow once and pass that specimen to both renderers.

## SVG

SVG is a botanical illustration, not a flattened screenshot of GL. For Plumeria it is also the canonical visual reference: improve GL toward its form, pigment and texture, never flatten the illustration to manufacture parity.

- Use one transparent colorway on light, dark, saturated and patterned grounds.
- Preserve true white through overlap and restrained edge separation rather than gray fill.
- Keep silhouette and primary color masses legible at 64 px.
- Let fine detail follow anatomy: clear pigment strokes and fine grain over soft washes, not a uniformly blurred surface.
- Keep a narrow contact shadow near the center so the five petals remain distinguishable without black outlines.
- Build the center from real organs and overlap, not an invented cover disk.
- Keep paint-critical properties inline so host CSS cannot repaint the flower.

## GL

GL is soft botanical tissue with readable volume.

- Broad organs keep rounded silhouettes and readable relief. Plumeria has soft lens-like volume and rounded rims, not bent flat sheets.
- Materials are opaque, matte and diffuse-dominant, without clearcoat or metallic glare.
- HDR light reveals curvature and overlap without bleaching pigment.
- Recessed grooves appear only where the species calls for them. Plumeria uses quiet, even-density isotropic micro-grain, not radial channels, stretched streaks or fur.
- Plumeria pigment follows each complete petal from base to tip, including its throat wash and halo. Do not project a flower-centered color effect across the corolla or bake a shaded ring into the pigment.
- Throat rays, veins and stripes are coloration, not incisions: avoid dark-core profiles that imitate a groove. They never change positions, normals or directional material relief. Named Candy Stripe specimens always retain a visible interior pink stripe in addition to their margin.
- Contact shadow comes from the actual overlap blocking light, not painted outlines. Plumeria uses gentle directional light to preserve depth at the central junction, without excessively dark overlap shadows. All five petals remain exact rotated copies; their illumination need not be identical.
- Internal softness remains restrained and follows tissue thickness.

Review clay GL before pigment. An isolated Plumeria petal must retain its entire narrow basal ribbon, progressively opening into the broad blade. The five petals wrap most tightly near their converging lower tips; together they close the basal outlet without a separate plug. Above, the throat stays recessed and the blades open mainly by widening and bending outward, with a quiet cyclic overlap rather than a propeller-like twist. Inspect the complete petal, assembled side and underside, and the basal closure—not only the flower's front. Then judge cultivar-defining pigment and the soft matte finish. Check unlit pigment and the colored isolated petal from multiple angles as well: attached paint must also read as coloration rather than simulated depth.

## Variation and review

A seed varies one plausible individual inside a cultivar. Large changes in color, structure or proportion belong to named cultivars, and published phenotype changes require a new revision.

Automation checks identity, geometry, bounds, repeated-organ reuse and SVG/GL correspondence. A maintainer still reviews botanical recognition, every cultivar, the named boundary cases, SVG at collection and hero sizes, and final and clay GL on light, gray and dark grounds.
